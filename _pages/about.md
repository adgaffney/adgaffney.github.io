I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in the Vascular Section of the Department of Surgery at The University of Chicago, advised by [Prof. Nhung Nguyen](https://surgbiomech.uchicago.edu/nhungnguyen/) of the [SurgBioMech Lab](https://surgbiomech.uchicago.edu/).

My research interests have evolved along the intersection of physics and biology throughout my academic career. I began studying experimental interfacial chemistry of lipid membranes in my undergraduate and early doctoral training. My late doctoral training focused on computationally modeling thin sheet physics applied to biologically relevant systems, introducing me to buckling instabilities, shape programming, and geometric manipulation of thin films. I developed a strong expertise in continuum mechanics and finite element (FE) modeling, while staying grounded in connections to experimental data and physical principles. As a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Surgery, I have expanded my computational capabilities to study tissue biomechanics and collaborate directly with surgeons. My current research projects focus on growth, stability, and biomechanics of tissues and organs, such as the aorta and diaphragm.
